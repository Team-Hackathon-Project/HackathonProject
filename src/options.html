Dashboard

Every ticker you are watching, on one page.

The copy inside the extension needs no server and no setup. Open it whenever you want the whole watchlist rather than the one tab you are looking at.

Optional. If you are running the dashboard as a website (npm run web), put its address here and the button above will open that copy with this extension's ID already filled in. Only localhost and 127.0.0.1 can reach the extension — that list lives in manifest.json and cannot be changed from this page.

Site access

Which quote pages the extension may re-read on its own.

Scanning a tab you are looking at needs no permission at all — that is what clicking the toolbar icon grants. Refreshing a ticker in the background is different: the page is not open, so the extension needs standing access to that one site. Nothing is granted at install, each host is asked for separately, and you can take any of them back here or from chrome://extensions.

Agent

Which model answers, and how much of the page it is allowed to see.

Both do the same two jobs — repair a broken selector, write an advisory. Pick whichever key you have.

No key? Get one here — or leave this empty and the extension keeps working from its built-in rules.

Stored in chrome.storage.local on this machine and sent only to the provider from the extension service worker. It is never exposed to the pages you visit. Keys are kept per provider, so switching does not discard the other one. Leave it empty to run fully offline: no self-healing, and advice comes from the local rules engine.

Test connection sends one tiny request, so you find out the key works here rather than mid-scan.

Portfolio targets

The rules engine and the LLM both read these. Targets are what turn a quote into a signal.

Only the ticker is required. Fill in buy below and sell above and the advisory has a threshold to reason about; leave them empty and every verdict comes back HOLD for want of anything to compare.

Suggest targets fills the two boxes from your own data — the average of the prices you have scanned for that ticker, or your approved BUY decisions, or your average cost — with a band taken from how much those scans moved. It proposes; you still press Save. Tick automatically and every later scan of that ticker refreshes them for you.
